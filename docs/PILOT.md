# Proposed farmer-outcome validation plan
Status: proposed only. No farmers recruited, no local agronomist review completed and no measured impact reported.

## Step 1 — Verify that the problem and workflow matter
Interview 5–8 consenting farmers and 2–3 local advisers in the intended plains region. These are recruitment targets, not completed interviews. Ask about the most recent rotation decision, actual water access, marginal pumping charges, buyer constraints, how advice is obtained, and the cost of trying a new crop. Observe current practice before showing the app; do not lead with desired benefits. Record sampling/recruitment limits and avoid generalising a small convenience sample.

Success evidence to seek: users can explain the decision they face; the presented trade-offs match their concerns; advisers can identify the specific local data needed. A usable interface or stated willingness to try is not proof of economic benefit or willingness to pay.

## Step 2 — Review the agronomy before changing a field
A qualified regional agronomist reviews crop portfolios, seasonal calendars, varieties, water coefficients, drainage, soil assumptions and known crop-specific pest constraints. Compare the annual model with daily/seasonal water balances and local observations. If the coefficients or rotation choices are unsuitable, revise or narrow scope before any planting recommendation. Do not have a farmer change crops on the prototype's score alone.

## Step 3 — Shadow decisions, without prescribing planting
Test the comparison during an actual farmer–adviser planning discussion. Log the farmer's original choice, constraints, assumptions, alternative considered, unresolved checks and final human decision. Measure time to a usable comparison and comprehension: can the farmer distinguish observed rainfall, a hypothetical scenario, a model proxy and a cost estimate? Check whether they interpret zero annual gap or a positive cost screen as a guarantee. Fix that misunderstanding before moving forward.

## Step 4 — Plan a supervised field evaluation
Only after agronomic review, choose a feasible trial design with the agronomist and farmers. Where appropriate, use comparable within-farm plots or a matched comparison group; record baseline soil/water conditions, irrigation method, variety, planting dates and management differences. Decide sample size through a justified power/feasibility assessment; do not invent a sample size that implies statistical reliability. Agree on stopping rules and farmer choice, and retain the existing practice where the alternative is unsuitable.

## Outcomes to measure
| Intended benefit | Direct measure | Important limitations |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lower water pressure | Metered or calibrated irrigation volume/ha, rainfall and timing; report irrigation method and conveyance losses | Lower volume alone can reflect crop stress or rainfall differences; record yield and seasonal adequacy |
| Better farm economics | Realised crop revenue minus all relevant input, energy, labour, irrigation and switching costs per ha | Report prices, yields, subsidies, fixed vs variable charges and uncertainty; do not transfer one farm's return to all users |
| A more useful planning conversation | Observed completion, comprehension and whether the saved option influenced a documented decision | Compare with the existing process; do not claim time saving from software responsiveness |
| Soil-management benefits | Baseline and repeat soil measurements with documented protocols; residue, cover and rotation management records | Multi-season changes may take years; proxy scores and short-term yield do not establish soil-health improvement |
| Crop diversity and pest implications | Actual crop sequence and crop-specific incidence/management records | Family changes do not by themselves demonstrate reduced pest losses or chemical use |
| Repeat use and accessibility | Consented follow-up use, adviser support required, language/device barriers and farmer feedback | Browser-local storage, literacy, language, connectivity and device access can limit uptake |

## Reporting
Predefine the outcomes and analysis before the trial. Report negative results, failed establishment, missing measurements, uncertain effects and costs borne by the farmer. Distinguish measured, modelled and self-reported evidence. Claims of water savings, income gains, soil improvement or resilience remain unavailable until appropriate evidence supports them.
